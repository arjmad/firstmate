2026-11-11: Arjun picked, in a helm grilling relayed by the main firstmate, version "A: by what it takes" of the calls-grouping design and accepted its three rules.
Decision: his calls are grouped by what answering takes - a tap, a board, a place, or his own hands - and a group is one held row.
A group names itself by structured fields only: `Batch: place:<name>` for one place's errand list, `Batch: board:<task-id>` for a board's members; a bare legacy tag reads as a place list, and an untagged call is quick when its hold records choices, else it takes his own hands.
Rule 1 (amends the 2026-10-27 and 2026-11-08 noticed rules): a noticed item is held for him only when it needs his decision or review; firstmate fixes or drops every other one, undoable only, and lists each in the Monday "Handled for you" list.
Rule 2: one answer on a group works all its members, a member never shows alone, and a call with its own outside deadline stays its own call.
Rule 3: a call is never re-asked and never closed by itself; held 14 days with no date, it shows once in the Monday triage under "Waiting 2+ weeks" with Keep, Drop or a date.
His 2026-11-01 rule stands: every call reaches him at once and none releases itself.
Why: it is the only version that cuts what he faces (14 lines tonight, 4 of them folded groups), and it matches how he works the pile off; workers' noticed rows were fixed as proposed 69% of the time, audit rows were dropped 42% of the time and never fixed, and 172 of 195 holds had no date.
